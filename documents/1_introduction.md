In recent years, the international financial environment has become increasingly sensitive to geopolitical developments, commodity market volatility, and sudden shifts in public perception. In this context, the ability to rapidly capture global changes in financial sentiment becomes essential for risk assessment, tension forecasting, and the grounding of economic policy decisions.

In this study, we construct a Global Financial Sentiment Index, designed to capture and synthesize public interest in financial terms associated with risk, uncertainty, and stress in international markets. The index is based on data extracted from Google Trends, which reflects the frequency of online searches at the global level for a set of financial terms formulated in English. Aggregated worldwide, online search data provides a complementary perspective to traditional indicators, capturing the immediate reactions of the public to geopolitical, economic, or energy‑related shocks.

Unlike traditional indicators, which are published with a delay, online search data offers a fast and flexible tool for monitoring global perceptions, as the data is updated daily by Google. To measure financial sentiment, we developed a fully automated system that extracts, processes, and aggregates normalized monthly data in Python. We then applied Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to the monthly percentage changes of the selected terms, with the aim of obtaining a synthetic indicator that reflects the aggregated dynamics of global financial sentiment. This approach enables dimensionality reduction and highlights the latent factor that best explains the common variation among terms associated with financial risk.

The Global Financial Sentiment Index provides an integrated perspective on how geopolitical, economic, and energy shocks are reflected in global public attention, serving as a useful tool for macroeconomic analysis, financial risk assessment, and monitoring of the international financial climate. The resulting index captures how populations react to major events — from energy crises and geopolitical conflicts to episodes of market calm and recovery.
