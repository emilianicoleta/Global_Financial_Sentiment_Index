Appendix 1. List of financial terms used in the construction of the Global Financial Sentiment Index

This appendix presents the complete list of financial terms used in building the Global Financial Sentiment Index. The terms were selected to capture relevant dimensions of financial risk, uncertainty, panic, and volatility perceived at the global level. The principal component analysis confirmed their relevance, highlighting a clear dominance of the term “oil price” in the structure of the PCA loadings.

1. The macroeconomic terms capture the general perception of the economy.  
   • economic growth – removed because the normalization of the time series generates unstable values due to extremely low variation, producing infinite values in percentage change calculations and affecting PCA stability.  
   • recession – removed for the same reason as above.  
   • GDP  
   • inflation  
   • deflation – removed because the average correlation is below 0,2.  
   • unemployment – removed because the average correlation is below 0,2.  
   • interest rate  
   • exchange rate  
   • monetary policy  
   • fiscal policy  

2. The financial stress and systemic risk are terms, which are essential for capturing panic and crises.  
   • financial crisis  
   • banking crisis  
   • credit crunch – removed because the average correlation is below 0,2.  
   • liquidity crisis  
   • sovereign debt crisis – removed because the average correlation is below 0,2.  
   • market crash  
   • stock market crash – removed because it has a correlation coefficient of 0,84 with market crash.  
   • bear market  
   • volatility  
   • VIX – removed because it is highly correlated with interest rate (coefficient above 0,9) and is a technical term specific to the U.S. market.  

3. The financial market comprises terms which capture public interest in stock market developments.  
   • stock market  
   • equities  
   • bonds  
   • commodities  
   • oil price  
   • gold price  
   • exchange rate volatility – removed because no data is available.  

4. The following geopolitical terms have financial impact.  
   • geopolitical risk – removed because no data is available.  
   • war – removed because the average correlation is below 0,2.  
   • terrorism – removed because the average correlation is below 0,2.  
   • sanctions  
   • oil shock  

5. The behavioral terms capture investor psychology.  
   • market sentiment – removed because it has a correlation coefficient above 0,9 with volatility.  
   • investor sentiment – removed because it has a correlation coefficient above 0,9 with volatility.  
   • fear index – removed because it is a technical term specific to the U.S. market.  
   • panic – removed because the average correlation is below 0,2.  
   • uncertainty  
   • risk aversion
