The first principal component explains approximately 48.2% of the total variance, which means that the Global Financial Sentiment Index captures nearly half of the common dynamics across all financial terms included in the analysis — a very strong outcome given the noisy and heterogeneous nature of Google Trends data. The index is cohesive rather than fragmented, indicating that it reflects a real latent factor that simultaneously influences the financial terms.

The eigenvalue of 504.99 shows that the principal component is statistically powerful and carries substantial energy, meaning the analysis has identified a genuine underlying “driver” of variation rather than an arbitrary combination of variables.

The structure of the PCA loadings highlights the contribution of each variable to the Global Financial Sentiment Index. When a variable’s loading is positive, the synthetic index increases in close connection with the public’s interest in that term on Google. When the loading is negative, the index decreases.

We conclude that the Global Financial Sentiment Index is driven primarily by shocks in the oil market, and secondarily by developments in equity markets. Positive shocks correspond to sharp increases in oil prices, while negative shocks correspond to sharp declines. The chart captures the economic narrative over February 2004 – June 2026, highlighting episodes of optimism (negative shocks) and pessimism (positive shocks), periods of stability and phases of heightened volatility.

Google Trends data reflects public attention, not necessarily economic fundamentals, because public perception (measured through search activity) reacts quickly to news, and search spikes may occur even without real changes. The constructed index provides a useful, fast, and complementary early‑warning signal alongside traditional indicators. It does not replace fundamental data, but it can enhance the ability to respond to energy shocks and sudden shifts in public perception.
